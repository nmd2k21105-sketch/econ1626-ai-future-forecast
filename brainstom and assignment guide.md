## Lessons From Assignment 2: What Must Improve

Assignment 2 scored 72. That is competent, but it is not enough for the Assignment 3 target. To avoid another 72-level result, Assignment 3 must improve in these areas.

### 1. Narrower Topic

Assignment 2 was too broad: AI and the Australian labour market generally.

For Assignment 3, avoid broad topics like:

- AI and Australia
- AI and jobs
- AI and the global economy

Use a narrow sector, task, institution, or policy setting instead.

Better structure:

```text
AI agents in [specific Australian industry/task] by 2027-2030
```

### 2. More Concrete Numbers

Assignment 2 used credible sources but did not use enough concrete figures inside the argument.

Assignment 3 needs:

- sector size
- number of workers or firms affected
- wages, costs, or productivity estimates
- adoption rates
- named programs
- dollar amounts
- measurable KPIs
- scenario assumptions that can be adjusted or visualised

Do not write only general claims. Put numbers in the body of the argument.

### 3. Stronger Causal Mechanisms

Assignment 2 had the right concepts but often stayed at a general level.

Assignment 3 must repeatedly show:

```text
AI capability -> task affected -> who changes behaviour -> who captures gains -> economic outcome -> policy response
```

Every major paragraph should answer:

- what exactly changes?
- why does it change?
- who benefits?
- who loses or bears risk?
- what institution or policy changes the outcome?

### 4. Better Source Integration

Assignment 2 had sources in the reference list, but some claims still felt unsupported or only loosely connected to the source.

Assignment 3 must:

- cite sources in-text for specific claims
- use evidence to defend the argument, not just decorate it
- explain why a source matters
- connect data to the forecast
- avoid long uncited stretches of analysis

Rule:

```text
If a claim matters, cite it.
If a number appears, cite it.
If a causal mechanism is borrowed from theory, cite it.
```

### 5. More Systematic Scenario/Policy Evaluation

Assignment 2 policy options were sensible, but the evaluation was not systematic enough.

Assignment 3 needs clear comparison across scenarios and policy choices.

Use explicit criteria such as:

- productivity
- employment/task displacement
- wage distribution
- firm concentration
- feasibility
- equity
- governance risk

Do not just describe options. Compare them.

### 6. More Precise Implementation Detail

Assignment 2 implementation was broad.

Assignment 3 policy playbook should include:

- named responsible institutions
- timeline
- measurable targets
- risks
- mitigations
- KPIs
- what triggers policy escalation or adjustment

Avoid vague language like "government should support workers" without saying how, who, when, and how success is measured.

### 7. Cleaner Writing And Professional Polish

Assignment 2 had some awkward phrasing and grammar issues that made the work feel less polished.

Assignment 3 needs:

- concise sentences
- fewer vague phrases
- no filler
- no awkward grammar
- no generic AI-style wording
- a confident, analytical tone

The writing should feel deliberate and edited.

### 8. Stronger Visual And Interactive Evidence

Assignment 2 was Markdown only. Assignment 3 is a web essay, so visuals matter.

Assignment 3 should include visuals that make the argument clearer, such as:

- scenario comparison chart
- task exposure matrix
- policy trade-off table
- timeline to 2030
- slider or toggle showing assumptions
- causal pathway diagram

Visuals must support the reasoning, not just decorate the website.

### 9. Better Git Process

Assignment 2 may have lost marks because the local repo only showed 3 commits, while the requirement was at least 5 meaningful commits.

Assignment 3 requires at least 10 meaningful commits.

Plan commits deliberately:

- commit topic and thesis
- commit source notes
- commit outline
- commit scenario model
- commit first draft
- commit visuals
- commit policy section
- commit references
- commit design polish
- commit final rubric audit

### 10. Stronger Originality

Assignment 2 was competent but not especially surprising.

Assignment 3 needs something more distinctive:

- unusual but defensible topic
- specific Australian evidence
- original visualisation
- clear forecast claim
- policy logic that feels tailored, not generic

The work should make Chris think:

```text
This student made specific choices and understood the economic mechanism.
```

## The Main Writing Rule

Every claim should trace a causal channel:

```text
AI capability -> task change -> firm/worker/institution response -> economic outcome -> policy implication
```

Do not just list impacts. Explain the mechanism.

Examples of mechanisms Chris mentioned:

- who captures productivity gains
- why the productivity-wage link breaks
- why some tasks are automated
- why other tasks are reinstated

## Concrete Beats Generic

Chris explicitly said:

> Concrete beats generic. Everyone can do generic.

For this assignment, concrete means:

- real sector data
- named programs
- dollar figures
- measurable targets
- task-level examples
- named institutions or firms
- specific Australian policy settings where relevant

## Evidence And Citations

Sources must be integrated into the argument, not just listed at the end.

Use in-text citations according to RMIT Harvard style.

Prefer:

- peer-reviewed papers
- official data
- government reports
- industry data from credible institutions
- course readings and lectures where directly relevant

Use blogs, media, and marketing pieces only as supporting context, not as the main evidence base.

## GitHub Pages And Technical Expectations

Chris will look for work that pushes what can be done on GitHub Pages.

This does not mean flashy decoration. The technology should serve the economic reasoning.

Good technical choices:

- interactive scenario comparison
- simple sliders for assumptions
- charts that show mechanisms
- timelines
- decision trees
- policy trade-off matrix
- clean layout that renders properly

Avoid:

- a single upload at the end
- broken GitHub Pages
- visuals that are only decoration
- placeholder charts
- messy design that distracts from the argument
