## Essay Overview

This interactive web essay forecasts how artificial intelligence will reshape Australian legal services between 2025 and 2030. The central argument is distributional: AI is automating the routine cognitive tasks that junior lawyers perform, and the productivity gains are concentrating among equity partners and AI vendors rather than being shared with workers or passed to clients.

**Sector:** Australian legal services ($35.5bn, 97,000 professionals)
**Forecast horizon:** 2025–2030
**Word count:** ~2,000 words

---

## Economic Argument

The essay traces a causal chain from AI capability to economic outcome:

1. LLMs perform legal research, drafting, and review at near-zero marginal cost
2. Junior associate routine tasks automated first (Autor, Levy & Murnane 2003)
3. Firms cut graduate intake; grow lateral senior hires (MinterEllison: −30.8%)
4. Productivity gains flow to equity partners and AI vendors — only 6% charge clients less
5. Market concentration increases: 74% of large firms have AI policies vs 25% of small firms

**Why the productivity–wage link breaks:** Worker wages depend on task scarcity, not firm productivity. When AI makes a task abundant, workers lose bargaining power even as the firm becomes more efficient (Acemoglu 2024).

## Interactive Features

All visuals serve the economic argument:

- Bar chart — AI substitution risk by seniority (displacement argument)
- Scenario tabs — Baseline / Upside / Downside (distributional forecast)
- Scenario comparison table — 6 criteria × 3 scenarios (systematic policy evaluation)
- Causal chain diagram — 5-node visual of the mechanism
- Task exposure table — maps legal tasks to AI exposure with evidence
- Adoption timeline — 2023–2026 firm-by-firm sequence
- Junior demand slider — interactive task automation model
