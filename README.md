## Essay Overview

This interactive web essay forecasts how artificial intelligence will reshape Australian legal services between 2025 and 2030. The central argument is distributional: AI is automating the routine cognitive tasks that junior lawyers perform, and the productivity gains are concentrating among equity partners and AI vendors rather than being shared with workers or passed to clients.

**Sector:** Australian legal services ($35.8bn, 97,000 professionals)  
**Forecast horizon:** 2025–2030

## Economic Argument

The essay traces a causal chain from AI capability to economic outcome:

1. LLMs perform legal research, drafting, and review at near-zero marginal cost
2. Junior associate routine tasks automated first (Autor, Levy & Murnane 2003; Eloundou et al. 2024)
3. Firms cut graduate intake; junior hours fall while senior hours rise (MinterEllison: −31%)
4. Productivity gains flow to equity partners and AI vendors — only 6% charge clients less (McKeith 2026)
5. Adoption gap persists by firm size: 52% of large firms use AI vs 17.4% of small firms (OECD 2025)

**Why the productivity–wage link breaks:** Worker wages depend on task scarcity, not firm productivity. When AI makes a task abundant, workers lose bargaining power even as the firm becomes more efficient (Acemoglu 2024). Total factor productivity gains remain modest (<0.55% over ten years) because full productivity requires organisational redesign, not just tool adoption (Berg 2026).

## Interactive Features

All interactive elements serve the economic argument directly:

- **Adoption timeline** — 2023–2026 firm-by-firm adoption sequence
- **Causal chain diagram** — 5-node visual of the displacement mechanism
- **Task exposure table** — maps six legal task types to AI exposure level with evidence
- **Junior demand slider** — interactive model letting readers stress-test displacement and reinstatement assumptions
- **Scenario tabs** — Baseline / Upside / Downside (with causal logic and trigger conditions)
- **Scenario comparison table** — four economic mechanisms × three scenarios
- **Policy timeline** — timed 2026 / 2029–30 recommendations with What to Avoid panel
